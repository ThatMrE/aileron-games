HIGGS

A Feynman-Diagram Deck-Building Card Game · 2 players · ~20–30 min

Two physicists race to reconstruct the Higgs boson. You draw particles from the Standard Model, collide them at real Feynman vertices, and climb the tech tree — light fermions → gluons → heavy quarks & vector bosons → Higgs. Every collision is a probability contest whose odds are the coupling constant of the force involved. Win the contest and you keep the initiative; assemble a Higgs-production vertex before your opponent and you win.

1. Overview & Objective

HIGGS is a competitive, interactive deck-builder for two players (or one player vs. the CERN AI in the digital version). It is "collaborative" in the physical sense that you build off each other's particles — every collision is an interaction between the two players' structures, just like a real Feynman diagram is read across two incoming lines.

  • Objective: be the first to complete a Higgs-production vertex in your beamline (reach 125 GeV).
  • Core loop: draw cards → collide particles → roll the coupling probability → the winner keeps the initiative and plays again.
  • Interaction: you can also disrupt your opponent by colliding an antiparticle into their beamline to annihilate a key ingredient.

2. The Physics It Teaches

Every mechanic is a real piece of the Standard Model, not just a theme:

  • Feynman vertices. Cards combine two-in / one-out exactly like a diagram vertex, where two particle lines meet a force carrier.
  • Coupling constants as probability. The chance a vertex "fires" is the interaction's coupling strength — strong » electromagnetic » weak.
  • E = mc². Making a heavy particle (top, W, Z) costs beam energy equal to its mass — and you only pay when the particle actually forms. You literally pay energy to create mass.
  • Yukawa coupling. The Higgs couples in proportion to mass, so the top quark (heaviest fermion) gives the best odds — which is why gluon fusion through a top loop is the real dominant production channel.
  • Antimatter & annihilation. A particle meeting its antiparticle annihilates into a boson — the basis of both production and disruption.
  • Generations & flavor change. The W boson changes quark/lepton flavor, letting you climb from first-generation matter toward the third.

3. Components

  • Particle deck — the Standard Model card set (quarks, leptons, bosons, antimatter). Each player starts with an identical 12-card Standard Model Starter.
  • Collider Market — a shared supply of stronger particles you acquire with energy (deck-building).
  • Energy tokens (⚡, "GeV") — beam luminosity; spent to create mass and to acquire cards.
  • Beamline — each player's growing Feynman diagram (the particles they've successfully produced).
  • Collision Chamber — the shared space where two selected particles resolve a vertex.

6. Coupling & Probability

The success chance of a vertex is its coupling constant — the number that governs how likely that interaction is in nature. The real values span a huge range, so HIGGS compresses them into a playable band while strictly preserving their ordering:

InteractionReal couplingGame odds
Strong (αs)~0.1 – 175%
Electromagnetic (α ≈ 1/137)~0.007355%
Weak (at low energy)strongly suppressed35–40%
Yukawa (yf ∝ mf)ytop≈1, yb≈0.024, yτ≈0.010scales with mass
Design note. Literal ratios (EM at 0.7%, weak far lower) would be unplayable, so we rank rather than reproduce them. What survives is the physics students should feel: the strong force fires reliably, the weak force is fickle, and the Higgs prefers heavy partners — the top quark most of all.

Luminosity boost (optional rule): spend 3⚡ before a roll to add +15% to that vertex's odds, representing higher beam luminosity buying more collision attempts.

7. Turn Structure

  1. Energize & draw. Gain +3⚡ (cap 20) and refill your hand to 5 cards.
  2. Collide (main phase). Choose two of your particles (hand and/or beamline) and resolve a matching vertex:
    • Success → you pay the vertex's energy cost (its rest mass), the product enters your beamline, and you keep the initiative — collide again (chain reactions). A successful strong collision also refunds +1⚡.
    • Failure → no particle is created, so no energy is spent; the hand cards used are discarded and initiative passes to your opponent. Beamline particles are never lost to a failed roll, so your structure is durable — you can re-attempt an expensive Higgs vertex on later turns without losing your ingredients or your banked energy.
  3. Disrupt (optional). Fire an antiparticle from your hand at one particle in the opponent's beamline (2⚡). On success it annihilates their ingredient. This is the primary way to block an opponent's path.
  4. Acquire (once per turn). Spend energy to buy a card from the Collider Market into your discard pile.
  5. End turn. Play passes. Cards cycle: when your deck empties, shuffle your discard into a new deck.
Initiative = tempo. Because a winning collision lets you go again, a hot streak of strong-force vertices can carry you a long way in one turn — exactly the "the winner of the interaction plays next" dynamic.

8. Win Conditions

These mirror the four real Higgs-production mechanisms at the LHC, roughly in order of how often nature uses them:

  1. Gluon Fusion (ggF) — g + g → H. The dominant real channel (~87% of LHC Higgs). Cheapest reliable win: make two gluons, invest 10⚡, roll 50%.
  2. Vector Boson Fusion (VBF) — W⁺ + W⁻ → H or Z + Z → H. Second most common (~7%). Requires building the weak bosons.
  3. Associated production / Higgs-strahlung (VH) — b + b̅ → H. Cheap (6⚡) but low odds (35%) — the gambler's route.
  4. Top-Associated (ttH) — t + t̅ → H. Rarest in nature (~1%) but the highest odds in-game (60%), because the top's Yukawa coupling is the strongest of all — if you can afford the 12⚡ and build a top + anti-top.

Alternate win — Deadlock: if an opponent cannot make any legal collision and has no cards left to draw, you win by default (their diagram has collapsed).

Blocking win: aggressive disruption can stall an opponent long enough that you complete your own Higgs first — the "block your opponent's structure" path from the brief.

10. Strategy & Balance

  • Gluon economy. Strong-force annihilations are free, 75%, and refund energy — the engine that funds everything. Flood your beamline with gluons early.
  • Two roads to gg-fusion. Two gluons + 10⚡ is the most efficient win. Everything else is a hedge or a flex.
  • The top gambit. ttH has the best Higgs odds but needs ~21⚡ total (two g+g→t at 9⚡ each, then 12⚡) — a late-game haymaker.
  • Deny with antimatter. Keep an antiquark in hand to annihilate the opponent's top or gluon right before their win roll.
  • Riding initiative. Chain cheap strong vertices to build tempo, then spend a whole turn's momentum on your Higgs attempt.
Rough balance target: games run 8–15 turns. First reliable Higgs window opens around turn 6–7 via gluon fusion; disruption pushes it later. Adjust the energy income (±1/turn) to speed up or slow down the whole game.
