// The Movement

Biology at the edge, in the open.

For too long, biotech has lived behind institutional walls — expensive, credentialed, closed. Biopunk is the counter-move: open-access labs, low-cost tools, and hands-on training so anyone can experiment, tinker, and build at the frontier of biology.

What started as a scrappy community lab has grown into an ecosystem — a place to run your first experiment, live alongside other builders, get a grant, ship a project in a weekend hackathon, or fork an open-source lab and drop it anywhere in the world. Much of it runs out of the 8th-floor biotech village at Frontier Tower, 995 Market St, San Francisco, and it’s spreading well beyond.

01Community Lab

Biopunk Lab

The flagship — the world’s largest open-access biolab. Shared equipment, low-cost bench space, and skill-building workshops so early-stage biotech projects can incubate. The home base at Frontier Tower.

02Residency

Biopunk House

A live-in residency for biotech founders — a home for biologists, hackers, and the wildly curious. DIY, rebellious, deeply collaborative, with access to a cutting-edge lab in San Francisco.

biopunk.houseVisit →
03Community Lab

Cellsius

The community bio lab that started it all in SF. Project-based learning, classes, and open experimentation across synbio, tissue engineering, DNA sequencing, robotics and more — the seed the wider community grew from.

cellsius.orgVisit →
04Microgrants

Primordia Grants

Fast microgrants — up to $3,000 — for early-stage biology experiments in community labs. No institution or credentials required. Grantees share their lab notes in the open and close each cohort with a public showcase.

primordiagrants.comVisit →
05Event

Biohackathon

A sprint of rebellious science at the intersection of synthetic biology, DeSci, and open science. Builders, hackers, artists and scientists prototype frontier biotech — DIY diagnostics, living art, lab automation — in days, not years.

biopunk-hackathon.vercel.appVisit →
06Open Hardware

Crab Lab

Open-source, modular shipping-container laboratories designed to be built anywhere. Blueprints and protocols are fully open, so any community can replicate a lab from local materials — science for remote and underserved places.

Biological Frontier Foundation

The nonprofit backbone of the community — advancing open, community-driven biology and supporting the labs, people, and projects working at the frontier of science.
