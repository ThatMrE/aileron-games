# Mondo 3000: Hell Rot — The Loading-Screen Deck (v1.0)

> Companion to [hellrot-design.md](hellrot-design.md) §8.8, which holds the
> deck's rules. This document holds the **cards**.
>
> Every load, blackout and 6 a.m. press run shows one card, styled as a
> *Mondo 3000* pull quote: the quote, its source, a context line where one is
> needed, and **Hell Rot's marginal note** in red pencil. 201 cards: 64 real
> quotations, 6 redacted cards, 106 in-world cards and 25 eye cards
> (18 powers, 7 portraits). Together they are the game's running argument with the Bay
> Area's oldest habit — building gods, and then selling tickets.

---

## How to read this deck

- **Tags** decide when a card can appear: `I1`–`I5` (Issue), `A`/`B`/`C`/`D`
  (route), a cult or character, and a mood (`funny`, `grim`, `holy`,
  `tender`, `weird`). Untagged by Issue means "any time."
- **"attributed"** marks a line that is widely credited to the speaker but has
  no single confirmed original source. Keep the word on the card.
- **REDACTED cards** show a leader's name, years and a black bar where a quote
  would be. These are the cult leaders whose words were used to lead people
  to their deaths. The game names them and refuses to give them a slogan.
  Hell Rot's note explains why, every time, in a different way.
- **Verification:** every real quotation goes through a source and legal check
  before release (main doc §8.8, rule 1). A card that fails the check is cut,
  never reworded into something the person did not say.
- **Recovery filter:** cards tagged `drugs+` are held back while Craving ≥ 3
  (main doc §5.6a). Cards tagged `recovery` take their place.

## Part 3 — Rotation rules (summary)

- **Weighting:** in-world cards are 60% of draws, real cards 40%. After a
  blackout, Karl and GIHKAL cards take half the draws.
- **No repeats** within one Issue. The redacted cards (X1–X6) appear at most
  once per playthrough each, and never back to back.
- **Eye cards:** an eye card enters the deck when its power is unlocked, and
  is drawn more often right after the power is used. Locked eye cards on the
  path the player is close to choosing appear once as a preview, tinted grey.
  If either eye reaches Corruption 3, a corrupt eye card replaces every fourth
  draw.
- **Route tagging:** route-tagged cards appear only on their route after the
  route lock.
- **Recovery filter:** `drugs+` cards hold while Craving ≥ 3. `recovery` cards
  double their weight while Craving ≥ 3.
- **Real-quote toggle:** turns Part 1 off and shows only Part 2.
- **The last card:** the final loading screen of every playthrough is always
  W88, unless the playthrough ends in Fabrication (ending 10), in which case
  it is a blank card with Yara's red pencil: *"Unverified."*

## Part 4 — Sources and verification notes

The real quotations above come from widely published sources. Before release,
the writers must confirm each against a primary or authoritative source and
cut any that fail. Notes:

- **Attributed lines** (R5, R8, R16, R36, R37, R56, R57, R60, R61) are widely
  credited but lack a single confirmed origin. The card must keep the word
  "attributed."
- **R11** (the "Frisco" decree) is commonly attributed to Emperor Norton, but
  historians doubt he wrote it. The card says so, on purpose.
- **R58** is a famous misattribution to Mark Twain. The card is about that.
- **R52:** William Powell, writing in *The Guardian* in 2013; the book's
  contents never appear in the game.
- **R51:** only the opening line of the manifesto appears, always with the
  harm named.
- **The redacted cards** contain no quotations by design.
- **Living people quoted** (Brand, Andreessen, Yudkowsky, the Founders Fund
  authors and others) are quoted briefly, accurately and in their published
  words. Hell Rot's notes argue with the *ideas*, never make claims about the
  people.
