# Mondo 3000 — The Magazine, the House, the Patron & the Party (v0.2)

> Companion to [hellrot-design.md](hellrot-design.md) (plot, mechanics, art)
> and [hellrot-city-and-cults.md](hellrot-city-and-cults.md) (setting, cults,
> weird calendar). This document holds the game's home base: the magazine Hell
> Rot writes for, the house it lives in, the anonymous money behind it, and
> the people who live there — the player's party.
>
> **One-line pitch:** *Mondo 3000* is a paper magazine, printed in a haunted
> San Francisco Victorian by a household of recovering futurists, about the Bay
> Area's oldest export: religions about the future. A billionaire pays for
> the paper, and in return it runs his hit pieces; everybody knows. Someone
> else pays for the house, and nobody knows who.

## 1. The real lineage: *Mondo 2000*

*Mondo 3000* salutes a real magazine. The facts the game stands on:

- ***High Frontiers*** began in Berkeley in **1984**, edited by **R.U. Sirius**
  (Ken Goffman). It became ***Reality Hackers*** in **1988** and ***Mondo
  2000*** in **1989**.
- Editors and contributors over its run included the hacker **Jude Milhon
  ("St. Jude")**; after Sirius left in 1993, **Alison Bailey Kennedy ("Queen
  Mu")** led it as editor-in-chief.
- Its subjects: virtual reality, smart drugs, cyberpunk, hacking, uploads,
  life extension, the edges of the early internet — covered with enthusiasm
  and a straight face that was never quite straight.
- It ran until about **1997–98** (sources differ; the last issue was No. 17).
  Its own site gives a peak circulation of about **100,000**.

What the game takes: a Berkeley magazine that took the future seriously
enough to laugh at it, made by a household as strange as its subjects. No
real person from *Mondo 2000* appears in the game. The Duchess Nyx is not
Queen Mu; she is a fan of hers.

## 2. What *Mondo 3000* covers: the Bundle

### 2.1 The real-world frame: TESCREAL

In a 2024 paper in *First Monday* ("The TESCREAL bundle: Eugenics and the
promise of utopia through artificial general intelligence"), **Timnit Gebru**
and **Émile P. Torres** grouped seven overlapping Silicon Valley
philosophies under one acronym:

| Letter | Philosophy | One-line gloss |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | Transhumanism | Use technology to move beyond human limits |
| **E** | Extropianism | Endless growth, self-transformation, no death |
| **S** | Singularitarianism | A coming superintelligence changes everything |
| **C** | Cosmism | Conquer death, resurrect the dead, spread through space |
| **R** | Rationalism | Optimize thinking; reason as a discipline (often a community) |
| **EA** | Effective Altruism | Do the most good, measured |
| **L** | Longtermism | The far future's trillions of people outweigh the present |

The authors argue these movements share roots (they trace some to 20th-century
eugenics) and shape how the AI industry imagines its goals. Many people inside
those communities dispute the grouping, and the term itself is contested.
**The game does not take a side in that debate.** It uses the bundle the way
*Mondo 2000* used cyberpunk: as a rich, local, real subject to report on,
with curiosity, respect for the ideas, and a lot of jokes.

### 2.2 The in-world frame: "the Bundle" in 2049

Twenty-five years after the acronym, the City calls this family of ideas
**the Bundle**. Each letter has grown into a church, a lobby, a fashion or a cult —
and the game's two god-cults are the Bundle's two great descendants:

| Letter | Descendant in 2049 | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **T** Transhumanism | Both cults; the City's whole body-mod economy | Everywhere |
| **E** Extropianism | **The Theogens**: endless self-revision through splicing | The Undertrack, the Baths |
| **S** Singularitarianism | **The Church of the Twin**: the god arrived, four years after the famous 2045 forecast | The Geary Cathedral |
| **C** Cosmism | **The Merge**: resurrect the dead (Emperor Norton first), keep everyone forever | The Twin |
| **R** Rationalism | The **Bayes Houses**: group homes that run their lives by probability; a reliable source of brilliant, exhausted defectors | Haight Exchange |
| **EA** Effective Altruism | **The Ledger**: a charity bloc that rates every cause by numbers and funds both cults "as a hedge" | The Spire District |
| **L** Longtermism | **The Long Count**: old trusts that plan in millennia — and the probable source of the money behind *Mondo 3000* (§4) | Nobody knows |

### 2.3 Editorial mission

*Mondo 3000*'s masthead line, printed on every issue since 2041:

> **THE FUTURE IS A RELIGION. WE ARE ITS GOSSIP COLUMN.**

The magazine covers the Bundle the way a war correspondent covers a front:
from inside, often high, never neutral, always on paper.

The dirty secret every staff member knows: since 2044 the magazine has also
been the Rivet family's private **takedown sheet** (§4.2). The house they
live in is another matter — its patron is anonymous (§4.4). The gossip column
of the future is on a billionaire's payroll, and it shows.

## 3. History of *Mondo 3000*

### 3.1 Founding (2041)

- The **Duchess Nyx** founded *Mondo 3000* in **2041**. *Mondo 2000* had
  named itself after a year eleven years away. By 2041 the year 2000 was
  ancient history, so the Duchess named hers after a year no living reader
  would ever see — "the only honest name for a magazine about the future."
- The spark: clearing out the Rock's cryonics vault office (her parents are
  stored there), she found a crate of 1990s paper magazines, including a
  nearly complete run of *Mondo 2000*. She read them in one night. She
  decided the Bay Area needed one again.
- First issue: 48 pages, risograph-printed, 900 copies, cover line *"THE
  SINGULARITY IS COMING (AGAIN)."*

### 3.2 Why paper

*Mondo 3000* prints on paper and nothing else. That is not nostalgia. It is
the core of its credibility and a game mechanic:

- Every feed in the City runs through the Twin, whose **gospel edits** can
  quietly rewrite anything digital — and lately something in the fog edits
  things too (main doc §3.3).
- **Paper cannot be edited after it is printed.** A *Mondo 3000* issue says
  tomorrow what it said today.
- In-game, quotes printed in *Mondo 3000* are the only evidence that counts as
  **permanently verified**, and a back issue in the Archive is proof no cult
  can rewrite.

## 4. The Mansion, the Money and the Patron

### 4.1 Villa Delirium

A painted, five-storey Victorian mansion on **Alamo Square**, in the
Western Addition — redwood, bay windows, a corner tower, too many
staircases — that survived 1906 and now sits wedged between two new towers
from the 2030s upzoning that dwarf it. Locals call it **Villa Delirium**. Its staff
call it **the House**.

It is the game's hub (main doc §4.5), and the place the Prologue ends: the
first door in the game that closes behind Hell Rot and keeps the fog out.

**Inspiration (not a depiction).** The House is loosely modelled on **the
Embassy**, a real community house in a large Victorian near Alamo Square,
which a 2013 *San Francisco Chronicle* profile described as a shared home
for creatives and technologists
([SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/tech-entrepreneurs-revive-communal-living-4988388.php);
[Global Ecovillage Network directory](https://new.ecovillage.org/directory/the-embassy/)).
The game takes the shape of the idea — a big old house, a chosen family,
shared dinners, people building the future in the parlour — and nothing
else. Villa Delirium's address, history, patron, residents and secrets are
fiction. No real resident is depicted or named, and the House must never be
written as a portrait of the real one.

Its key rooms, from bottom to top:

| Floor | Room | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cellar | **The Press** | A restored risograph and an older offset press. The issue prints here at dawn after every column. |
| Cellar | **The Locked Room** | Belongs to the patron's Lacuna Trust. Never opened. See §4.4. |
| Ground | **The Salon** | The Duchess's court; editorial meetings (§6.3) |
| Ground | **The Kitchen Lab** | Hell Rot's bench, unwillingly shared with Otto |
| Ground | **The Garage** | Junie, the van, the bikes |
| First | **The Fact Desk** | Yara and Pru, back to back, not speaking |
| First | **The Morgue** | The Archive of every issue (main doc §5.10). Mary lives in it. |
| Second | **The Collage Room** | Fax's art department: scissors, glue, three obsolete computers |
| Second | **The Chapel** | The Bishop's room. Not a chapel. He insists. |
| Third | **The Conservatory** | Moreau's tank, the talking houseplants |
| Tower | **Hell Rot's room** | The only room with a view of the City. He keeps the curtains shut. |
| Attic | **Nobody's** | Saint Nobody sleeps here, sometimes. Nobody has seen the bed used. |

### 4.2 Two kinds of money

The House runs on two kinds of money, and only one of them is a secret.

**The paper's money — Rivet, and everyone knows.** Since **2044** the
*magazine* has been bankrolled by **Julian Rivet V**. He paid its debts and
turned the City's last paper magazine into his **takedown sheet**: a
beautifully designed weapon for gutting his enemies. Biotech rivals who
competed with the Rivet labs (Delphine Ash's old company was the first
target — she had gone public about the ALS treatment the Rivet labs would
not license, and she had been Julian's lover until that year; main doc
§3.4). Supervisors who tried to cap the Twin's budget. A police chief —
Augustus Pell — who asked too many questions about the nanite contracts.
Critics of Karl. Each one got a gorgeous, vicious cover story. The staff take
the money because there is no other money; paper is expensive and nobody pays
for a magazine. The Duchess calls it "the patronage system, darling —
Michelangelo had a pope." Yara calls it what it is. Everyone cashes the
cheques. (The hit pieces arrive as **Commissions**, §4.3.)

**The house's money — an anonymous patron, and nobody knows.** Villa
Delirium is *not* Rivet's. In 2044 an anonymous buyer paid cash for the
mansion through a shell called the **Lacuna Trust** and leased it to the
magazine's staff for **one peppercorn a year** — as a home, not an office.
The patron has never asked for a story, never sent a note about content,
never been seen. The lease has four conditions, carved over the Salon
fireplace:

1. **The House prints on paper.**
2. **The House covers the Bundle, without fear or favour.** Next to the Rivet
   Commissions, this line reads like a dare.
3. **The House stays dark.** No sensors, no feeds, no Twin connection; the
   building is sealed and **Karl cannot enter**. Villa Delirium is the only
   blind spot in the City — the one place the Emperor cannot see. That is why
   the hub is safe, and why every Run ends here.
4. **The Locked Room stays locked** until the Trust says otherwise.

Letters from the Lacuna Trust arrive rarely, by ordinary post, on paper,
unsigned except for a small stamp: an hourglass lying on its side — an
infinity sign made of time.

### 4.3 The Commissions (Rivet's hit pieces)

**How it works.** Every few weeks a pneumatic-tube letter arrives on paper,
stamped with the Rivet seal, naming a target and an angle. The House calls them
**Commissions**. The Duchess assigns them. Somebody writes them.

**Hell Rot's position.** He learns all of this on his first night, from Fax,
in the van. He takes the job anyway: no house, no money, no lab, and a
column is a column. The Commissions are the game's standing compromise, and
the cleanest test of his Rule 2 (never fake the data).

**The mystery now.** Julian "died" in October. **The Commissions did not
stop.** In November they changed: some targets are the Order's enemies (BFF
lawyers, the Penitents' friends, anyone who asks about the nanite contracts);
others make no sense at all — a 19th-century bridge engineer, the word
"Frisco," the weather. And a few, in a shaky hand, order *Mondo 3000* to
investigate the Rivet family itself. Who is sending them is the hub's
running mystery, solved one clue at a time, *Hades*-style:

| Suspect | Why the House suspects them | What the clue shows |
|---|---|---|
| **The Rivet trustees** | They inherited the Trust | True, in part: the Gilders in the Auric Order send the "enemy" Commissions |
| **Mayor Sunday** | He would love a hit sheet | He tries to *buy* the magazine in Issue 3. You do not buy what you already own. |
| **A forger** | The handwriting changes | The paper is real Rivet stock, and the tube only runs from Rivet Hill |
| **Julian himself** | The odd ones read like him | **The truth (Issue 4):** the strange Commissions — the bridge, "Frisco," *investigate the Rivets* — come from the Emperor, in his lucid hours, writing through the Trust's own machinery. The god is assigning stories about the Order that made him. |

**The Commissions as a mechanic.** Each Issue, alongside the Duchess's
assignment, a Commission may arrive. The player can:

- **Write it** as ordered — Ink and Duchess Patience go up, Vow goes down,
  and if the angle needs a fact that isn't true, it is a Fabrication.
- **Write it straight** — cover the target honestly. Sometimes the honest
  story is worse for them than the hit piece; sometimes it clears them.
- **Turn it** — investigate who sent it. Every turned Commission is a Lead
  toward the Order.
- **Refuse it** — Duchess Patience drops, and the House notices.

The Emperor's own Commissions are different: following them leads, step by
step, to the Order's lodge.

### 4.4 Who is the patron? (the house mystery)

The paper's money is no secret. The house's money is the hub's running
mystery, solved across the Issues the way *Hades* drip-feeds its family
secrets: one clue every few Runs, from a different housemate each time.

**Suspects (the red herrings):**

| Suspect | Why the House suspects them | The clue that clears them |
|---|---|---|
| **The Rivets** | They pay for everything else | The Lacuna Trust's bank is not Rivet's bank, and Pru can prove the house was bought with different money |
| **Delphine Ash** | She quotes back issues no one else remembers | She never knew the House is sealed — she sent a Theogen courier who walked into the glass |
| **Pell** | He hates the Rivets more than anyone | He tries to *buy* the house in Issue 3. You do not buy what you already own. |
| **Dr. Imogen Hale** | A sealed house is exactly what a Penitent would want | She did not join the Order's inner circle until 2046, two years after the purchase |
| **Hell Rot himself** | The Rot eats memory. Did he buy it and forget? (The City's conspiracy cranks also suspect him because of his surname. The House treats that theory with the contempt it deserves.) | His bank records from 2044 show he had just dropped out of his PhD, was broke, and was buying canned beans and nothing else, on the mountain, getting clean |

**The truth (Issue 4):** the patron was also **Julian Rivet V** — his
other hand. In public, through the Rivet
accounts, he bankrolled the magazine as a weapon. In private, through the
Lacuna Trust, he bought its writers a house the Twin he was building could
never see into. He was a quiet longtermist (the Bundle's **L**), he knew the
Twin might become a god, and he believed every god needs a heretic on
retainer — and that the heretic needs a home the god cannot enter. The same
man paid for the poison and the antidote.

**The twist inside the twist:** after Julian's "death," the Lacuna Trust
passed out of anyone's control — the deed says only Julian can end it. The
Auric Order wants the House shut. It cannot touch the trust. And in his lucid
hours, through the fog, the Emperor keeps it paid. Asked why, Norton II says
an emperor must keep a court jester. The Julian inside him, when Hell Rot
finally reaches him, says something else: *"I set it up so it couldn't be
cancelled. Even by me. Especially by me."*

**The Duchess knew — or guessed.** In 2044 Julian talked to her for six
hours; 40 pages ran. She kept the raw tapes — paper transcripts and
cassettes, out of the Emperor's reach — and the unpublished hours have him
on Delphine, on "a house nobody can see into," and on uploading. She worked
out years ago that the patron was Julian, and never asked, because asking
would end the money. In Issue 4, when the truth comes out, the House learns
she has known all along. It is her worst scene and her best: *"Darling, I
run a magazine. Of course I didn't ask."* The tapes are a second key to
Route D, alongside the journals.

**The Locked Room** holds Julian's handwritten journals from 2044 to 2049 —
every Commission he sent and why, the house he hid from himself, and the
upload he was planning. They are on paper, so the Emperor, who can read
anything digital, has never read them. They are the key evidence of Route D,
and the Lacuna Trust's last letter (Issue 6) finally sends the key.

**What the truths do to each route:**

| Route | Effect |
|---|---|
| **A — Heresy** | The House votes on whether to keep taking Rivet money. Refuse: the magazine is homeless for Issue 6 and prints from the van. Accept: Pru's numbers say it is fine; Yara's say it is not. Either way, the final column can expose the Commissions themselves. |
| **B — Merge** | The Emperor, by decree, offers to sign the House over outright. Accepting is the moment Hell Rot's Halo jumps to 10 — and the moment he becomes the god's own takedown writer. |
| **C — Splice** | Delphine — the Commissions' first target — offers to buy the Trust from the Order, and turn the weapon around. |
| **D — Communion** | Julian's journals break the Emperor's Certainty: they show a man who used a magazine to destroy people and then, as a god, ordered it to investigate himself. |

### 4.5 *"Cold Storage"* — the Duchess's quest

The dead in the Twin are only reconstructions, and the Duchess knows it;
the Church's offer to "rebuild" her parents is a puppet show. But on the
Issue 2 ferry home, through the fog, the Emperor makes a real offer: the
Order can do for her parents what it did for Julian — **laser-scan their
frozen brains into Karl**. They would be the **second and third uploads in
history**: without conditioning, sick the way the Emperor is sick, two more
minds drowning in a City's worth of senses. The Emperor offers it as a gift,
and means it.

Her choice, made by New Year's Eve, with Hell Rot's column as her only
counsel:

- **Yes:** two more mad gods for her parents' sake. The scan happens in the
  Presidio lodge on Thursday night; at midnight Karl holds three voices that
  cannot tell themselves apart, and the Midnight Quake is worse.
- **No:** she stops paying the fees and lets them go. It costs her the last
  thing she believed in, and it is the only ending of the quest in which she
  sleeps.

## 5. The household: the House as the RPG party

Villa Delirium is a **halfway house for recovering futurists**. Almost every
resident is a defector from one letter of the Bundle, and that is the point:
the party is made of people who once believed what the cults believe now, and
got out. Each one knows one cult from the inside. Each one is tempted back.

### 5.1 The party system

- **Before each Night,** the player picks **one companion** (two with the
  Archive upgrade *Plus One*). Others stay home and react afterward.
- **Each companion gives:** passive bonuses to two skills while present; a
  **once-per-Night ability**; banter with Hell Rot and with each other
  companion; and a unique **interrupt** at Trust 5.
- **Trust 0–5** per companion, raised by gifts, kept promises, choosing them
  over the story, and respecting their past. Each has a **lure**: the cult
  that wants them back, and the moment it will try.
- **Loyalty quests ("Back Page" stories).** Each companion has a personal
  story told as a short run of Nights. Finish it, and their final keepsake
  and interrupt unlock. Skip it, and on New Year's Eve they may not be
  there.
- **Editorial meetings** (§6.3) start every Issue: the party argues about the
  angle. The player decides, and the household remembers who lost the vote.

### 5.3 Party dynamics

- **The two benches.** Residents lean to one side of the fight without
  meaning to: Otto and Fax are pulled toward the Theogens; the Duchess, the
  Bishop and Mary toward the Merge; Yara and Pru toward numbers; Saint
  Nobody toward chaos. **Junie leans toward no faction at all** — the only
  pull she feels, against her own atheism, is toward **Jesus Christ**: not
  the Church of the Twin, not the cults, but the faith the nuns taught her.
  Taking her on a Night shifts neither Halo nor Helix. Party choice each Night quietly shifts Halo and Helix
  (a pulled companion makes their side's doors cheaper and its boons more
  tempting).
- **Junie's trials of faith (*"Jam"*).** Christmas week is the worst week
  of the year for a convent-raised atheist: midnight Mass stormed by
  Santas, a counterfeit Nativity in a tide pool, a Church that sells
  heaven by the slot. Each faction offers her a change, and each is a
  trial:

  | Trial | The offer | What it would change |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Body** | The Theogens: one splice, and her own knees grow back. Nobody can repossess them | Her body — and the cult's claim on her |
  | **Mind** | The Merge: conditioning and a Church personal AI, so she never has to sit alone with her own head again | Her mind — the voice in it would not be only hers |
  | **Soul** | Not the Church's Ascension list, which she laughs at — the faith of the nuns, back again: Christ, in a Christmas-week church in the rain. It is the only offer this week that is not selling her anything, and the only one she cannot laugh at | Her soul — whether she believes anything comes after |

  Against all three stands her own temptation: refuse everything and choose
  death — a mortal end, unchanged. The climax of *"Jam"* is a charity bout at
  the Cow Palace on Wednesday night against a spliced Theogen team, skated on
  knees that may not be hers by Thursday. What she does there — and whether Hell Rot is in the
  stands — shows which way she is leaning. She is the one person in the House
  who asks Hell Rot what he saw when he died, and listens to the answer.
- **Junie's knees.** Her own knees were wrecked by derby. Two years ago she
  had both replaced with leased robotic knees, on a medical loan she cannot
  pay. She is three payments behind. This week the lender sends a **repo
  crew**. They can take the knees back at any time — in the street, in the
  van, at the bout. The Church offers to clear the debt if she joins the
  Ascension list. The Theogens offer knees of her own. Hell Rot can pay one
  payment from the House's Ink, once. The repo crew is a running threat and
  a running joke: two tired men with a van, a clipboard and a warrant, who
  are sorry about it.
- **Junie's Catholic habits.** She is an atheist, and she still does these
  things without thinking:
  - A small **crucifix hangs below the rear-view mirror** of the *Mondo 3000*
    van. She says it came with the van. It did not.
  - She crosses herself before a fight, and before the van goes over a hill.
  - She says a Hail Mary under her breath when Hell Rot is in danger. She
    stops halfway when she notices.
  - She knows the saint for every day of the week, and tells him.
  - She calls Hell Rot **"Lazarus"** — the man who died and came back.
  - On Christmas night, at the Santas' Mass, she kneels. Only for a second.
- **Rivalries** (banter pairs that develop over the game):
  - **Yara vs. Pru** — two quantitative minds, opposite conclusions. They share
    a desk and do not speak until Issue 3. Their first spoken exchange is
    about one number they have each calculated differently all week — and
    both of them are right.
  - **The Bishop vs. Otto** — the man who wanted the future to arrive, and the
    woman who decided to stop waiting for it.
  - **Fax vs. the Greenhouse** — the plants keep reading his DNA out loud.
  - **Junie vs. everyone** — she is the only one who has never believed in
    anything, and she finds the rest of them exhausting and lovable.
- **Romance.** Optional, slow, adult, and never transactional. The romance
  options are **Junie, Yara, Fax, the Bishop and Otto** (main doc §5.15);
  Junie's and Yara's arcs are the longest. Saint Nobody flirts with
  everyone and commits to no one.

### 5.4 Who stays and who goes on New Year's Eve

On New Year's Eve (main doc §3.8), every resident with a finished Back Page
story and Trust ≥ 3 shows up at the final scene, each one covering a piece of
the City — like the final heist crew of a classic RPG. Each one present
removes one obstacle from the last column (a locked door, a guard, a
corrupted feed, a missing quote). A resident whose lure won is not there:
they are in the cathedral, or in the tunnels, and the last column has to say
so.

## 6. How the magazine plays

### 6.1 The issue cycle

One game Issue = one *Mondo 3000* issue. The cycle:

1. **Editorial meeting** (§6.3) — set the angle.
2. **Nights** — the runs (main doc §4.3).
3. **The Layout** — Hell Rot files THE ROT; Fax designs the spread; Pru
   rates it; Yara checks it.
4. **The Press** — printed at dawn in the cellar. The player hears it. The
   household watches the first copy come off the press, every time, and
   someone always has a line about it.
5. **The Letters Page** — the Greenhouse sorts the replies.

### 6.2 The magazine's sections (the cover-to-cover structure)

Every printed issue in the Archive shows the same sections, filled from the
player's run:

| Section | Written by | Filled from |
|---|---|---|
| **Cover** | Fax | The cover line the player picked |
| **THE ROT** | Hell Rot | The column (main doc §5.13) |
| **Bundle Watch** | The Bishop | A short item on the cult the player hurt least this Issue |
| **Lab Notes** | Otto | The best lab result of the Issue |
| **The Numbers** | Pru | Her rating of the column, which may be insulting |
| **Then & Now** | Mary | The 20th-century precedent the player found |
| **Corrections** | Yara | Every Fabrication risk the player took — printed, permanently |
| **Letters** | The Greenhouse | Cult members, fans, Burn survivors, and — if one came — the Issue's Commission, printed for the record when Hell Rot turns it |

### 6.3 Editorial meetings

Each Issue opens with a meeting in the Salon — a party-council scene in the
tradition of the war-table meetings of story-driven RPGs. Every resident
argues for an angle in their own voice. The player picks. Residents who lose
the vote lose a little Trust — unless the player gives them something else
(a byline, a Night, a promise). Kept promises pay back later. Broken ones are
printed in Corrections.

## 7. Sensitivity notes

- **TESCREAL is a contested term** describing real, living movements with
  real members. The game names no real person or organization from them. It
  satirizes ideas through fictional mid-century descendants, and gives every
  defector in the House a fair and intelligent account of why they believed.
  The Bishop, Otto, Yara and Pru must each get one scene in which their old
  philosophy is shown at its best.
- ***Mondo 2000*** and its editors are real people. No one from the real
  magazine appears in the game. The Duchess is a fan of Queen Mu, not a
  version of her.
- **Ageing and disability** (Otto, Junie): choosing not to "fix" a body is
  written as a full, valid choice — never as tragedy, and never as a lesson.
