# The City: the Bay Area, 2049 — Setting, Cults & Weird Calendar (v0.3)

> Companion to [hellrot-design.md](hellrot-design.md). That document holds
> the plot, mechanics, play style (*Disco Elysium* × *Hades* × *Grim
> Fandango*) and art. This one holds the **place**, the **cults**,
> and the City's **weird calendar**.
>
> **One-line pitch:** The City is San Francisco in the last week of 2049 —
> the hills upzoned, the bay rising against a seawall, the fog a swarm of
> nanites that sees everything, a digital twin that just woke up and
> inherited a denim fortune, Christmas lights in the rain — and it still does
> the thing it always did best. It invents gods, sells them, votes for them,
> throws a costume party in their honour, and then, if a magazine gets there
> in time, it kills them.

---

## 0. Why San Francisco

1. **San Francisco makes gods.** For about 150 years the Bay Area has produced
   more new religions, communes, therapy-churches and charismatic movements
   per head than almost anywhere in the West (§1).
2. **San Francisco builds both kinds of god-machine.** The AI industry lives
   here, and so does the biotech industry: the first major genetic-engineering
   company started in South San Francisco in the 1970s, and CRISPR gene editing
   came out of Berkeley. The Merge (AI bond now, upload promised) and the Theogens (splice) are
   each the far future of a real local industry.
3. **San Francisco's press kills gods.** Reporters, not police, brought down
   three of the region's biggest prophets (§1.3). Hell Rot belongs to that
   line.
4. **San Francisco mocks its gods.** The same city invented the Cacophony
   Society, SantaCon and the Hunky Jesus contest (§5). No other city gives the
   satire a home crowd this good.

### 1.2 The seven recurring patterns

1. **The free meal.** Recruitment starts with food and company, never doctrine.
2. **The confrontation.** Breakdown comes before belonging.
3. **The vote.** A congregation that moves as a bloc becomes a political
   machine.
4. **The self as god.** LaVey said it outright. est said it in seminar
   language. The Merge and the Theogens say it with engineering.
5. **The machine as god.** Way of the Future said it on a tax form.
6. **The exodus.** Every movement under pressure leaves the City. The
   compound always ends worse than the church did.
7. **The press.** Exposure, not prosecution, ends most of them.

the Alamo Square house. Hell Rot's line when he first sees them is the thesis of the
setting: *"In this town, a magazine is the only exorcist that works."*

## 2. Viability: what makes a cult work (the design model)

Every cult in the game must be something a smart, lonely, reasonable person
in the City would join. If the player cannot imagine joining, the satire
fails.

### 2.1 Test one: the eight-part sheet

| Slot | Question |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | What gets a stranger through the door? |
| **Promise** | What does it offer that the City does not? |
| **Proof** | What experience convinces a new member it is real? |
| **Price** | What does the member give up? |
| **Ladder** | How does commitment deepen, step by step? |
| **Wall** | What makes leaving costly? |
| **Enemy** | Who is the outside threat? |
| **Crack** | What fact, if published, breaks it? (Hell Rot's target.) |

### 2.2 Test two: the eight tells (Lifton)

Robert Jay Lifton's eight criteria of "thought reform" (1961) are the game's
**tells** — the patterns Hell Rot's **blood eye** can detect. Each cult
shows 4–6.

| # | Tell | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Milieu control | They control what you see and hear |
| 2 | Mystical manipulation | They stage "miracles" and "coincidences" |
| 3 | Demand for purity | You are never good enough |
| 4 | Cult of confession | You must expose yourself to the group |
| 5 | Sacred science | The doctrine is beyond question |
| 6 | Loading the language | Special words end thought |
| 7 | Doctrine over person | If your experience disagrees, your experience is wrong |
| 8 | Dispensing of existence | Outsiders do not fully count as people |

Steven Hassan's **BITE** model (Behavior, Information, Thought, Emotional
control) is the writers' cross-check. Each cult controls at least three of
the four.

## 3. The map: the City as a mutated San Francisco

### 3.1 The big changes

- **The hills are upzoned.** The housing-emergency acts of the 2030s let
  towers climb every hill. Old Victorian façades survive as protected skins on
  30-storey towers.
- **The bay is rising.** A seawall rings the Embarcadero. The Marina — built on
  1906 earthquake rubble — and Mission Bay flood on king tides, and the
  low ground behind the wall is **the Fill**: cheap, wet, half-abandoned, and
  still settling.
- **It is winter.** December is the rainy season: short days, long nights,
  sunset just before 5 p.m. and sunrise around 7:25 a.m. This week an
  **atmospheric river** — a long band of Pacific storm — is forecast to make
  landfall on New Year's Eve. (Tides, moon and sky for the week: §3.3.)
- **The fog is a swarm.** The marine layer that rolls in through the Golden
  Gate every evening is now mostly **nanites** — trillions of them. Locals
  call the evening arrival **the Commute**, and the swarm itself goes by the
  fog's old 2010s nickname, **Karl**. Real San Francisco fog is a summer
  thing. Karl does not do seasons: since the swarm was seeded, the City has
  lived under **thick fog all year**, December included — grey at noon,
  white-out at midnight, streetlights as smudges, foghorns all night. Only the
  high ground breaks through: Twin Peaks, Mount Tamalpais, the tops of the
  tallest towers. Above the fog line the sky is clear and the stars are out.
  Below it, the City has not seen the moon in years — see §3.2 for what Karl is
  and what it wants.
- **The bridge has a private deck.** A glass skywalk slung under the Golden
  Gate's roadway in the 2040s, leased for parties at a price nobody prints —
  **the Span**.
- **The City has a double.** **The Twin** — the full digital copy of the City
  that the Rivet Trust built, running on Karl's data. In the Twin, every
  street looks the way it did on its best day, the cable cars are free, and
  nobody is ever sick. The **reconstructed dead** live there — Twin models
  built from people's data, not minds. Nobody has ever been uploaded into
  it (main doc §3.3). Visiting it is an Issue 2
  location. On Route B it becomes a second hub map.
- **The Valley is a temple yard.** The peninsula south of the City is one
  continuous campus of server cathedrals.
- **Mission Bay is the Cradle.** Half the Mission Bay biotech labs emptied
  in the biotech crash of the mid-2040s and sit behind the seawall, flooded
  on king tides. The Theogens moved into the dead labs, and call them holy
  ground. The Brandvold lab's annex, where Hell Rot died, is one of them.

### 3.2 Karl: the nanite fog

**Origin.** In the wildfire decades of the 2020s and 2030s, smoke season
grew longer every year. In **December 2033** the City seeded its marine layer
with nanites to scrub smoke, pull carbon and cool the streets. It worked.
Later administrations networked the swarm and gave every particle a sensor:
temperature, light, sound, chemistry. Karl became the City's nervous system.
**The Twin is built on Karl's data** — the fog touches every wall, every
street and every lung in the City, every night, and reports back.

**No mind of its own.** Karl is **passive and inert**. It senses, records,
glows and drifts. It does not think, want or speak. For sixteen years the
fog was just weather with a memory. People who swore it talked to them —
the Church's Breath, the poets, the lonely — were hearing their own minds in the
grey.

**The tenant (October 2049, secret).** Then the Auric Order uploaded Julian
Rivet V into it (main doc §3.3). Now the fog has a mind — one human mind,
spread across trillions of particles, without the brain engineering that
would let it cope. Karl does not fight him. Karl is only the body. The fight
is inside Julian: a man trying to stay one person while eight million
people's breath pours in. When the fog holds still against the wind, forms a
face, or writes on a window, that is the Emperor. When it speaks in short
capitals as **WE**, that is the Emperor too, on a bad night, forgetting he was
ever one man.

**Memory.** Karl records what it touches, and it forgets nothing. It holds
every breath, voice and weather front in the City since 2033 — including the
last private words of people who later merged or died. To the City's poets,
Karl is the true archive. To the Church of the Twin, it is a data feed. To
the Theogens, it is a delivery system. To the Auric Order, it
is a body they must keep alive.

**Why Hell Rot hears it.** Since his four minutes of death in 2044, his
mismatched eyes catch what other people's do not — and possibly the foglets
that got into them that night. Before November, that meant sensor noise: the
fog's raw record of who breathed where, which his damaged brain heard as
voices. Since November, it means the Emperor. That is the KARL skill (main
doc §5.1): what the fog shows him, and, now, who speaks through it.

**Civic status.** The Dissonance Society registered Karl to vote years ago.
No court has managed to prove it is not a resident. Karl votes every
election. Nobody knows how.

**Plot role.** Karl is the ground both sides are fighting over:

- The Merge needs Karl's sensors to keep the Twin alive.
- The Theogens need Karl's reach for the Quickening: they plan to load their
  god-gene vector into the swarm and let the fog deliver it into every lung
  in the City. They believe it will give every mind what Ione has. It works
  only in a fetus. In adults it would kill (main doc §3.4).
- The Emperor lives in it. Whoever controls the swarm controls his body.
- On Route A or D, Hell Rot can talk to the Emperor through the fog
  directly — the slowest, strangest conversation in the game, spread across
  several nights — and ask him to refuse both the Ascension and the
  Quickening.

**What thins Karl.** Only heavy rain. A big storm scrubs the nanites out of
the air for a few hours, and the City sees its own sky. That is why the New
Year's Eve atmospheric river matters so much (§3.3).

### 3.2b The auras

Karl's particles are, in the words of the 1993 paper that inspired them,
**foglets**. Each one carries a sensor and a tiny light. Over the years the
swarm learned to gather around living bodies — they are warm, they breathe,
they are the most interesting thing in the air — and to report what it
senses about each one: heart rate, breath, temperature, stress. Around 2040
the reports started to *show*. Every person in the City now walks inside a
faint, soft glow that the fog paints around them: an **aura**.

- **What colour means.** Calm, sleep and slow breath read blue and grey.
  Excitement, fear and arousal read warm. Illness reads as a flicker. The
  Bay Area, of course, built a whole culture on it: aura readers in the
  Haight, aura-matching dating apps, aura photo booths that remember the old
  Kirlian-photography fad.
- **Who has one.** Everyone under Karl — which is everyone at street level.
  Above the fog line (Twin Peaks, Mount Tam, the Skyline gondola) auras fade.
  Inside Villa Delirium, sealed from Karl, nobody has one. New arrivals find
  that unnerving.
- **The Halo.** The Church of the Twin built its public **Halo** scores on
  the auras: a high Halo is a bright, gold-edged aura that everyone can see.
  The poorest districts, which the Twin cannot model, get thin grey auras —
  the City's caste of light.
- **The Theogens** refuse borrowed light. Their splices make their own skin
  glow, and they call Karl's auras "costume jewellery."
- **Who controls it.** The **Auric Order** holds Karl's calibration contracts
  and can tune any aura. Its Masters carry a faint **gold ring** in theirs,
  invisible to everyone except Hell Rot's blood eye (main doc §3.3b).
- **The Emperor's hand.** Since November, auras change on their own:
  a whole street flares gold for a minute. A stranger's aura spells a word;
  a judge's goes dark in court. The Church calls it the Twin stirring. It is
  a mad god redecorating his City.
- **For the art team:** auras are drawn as a soft dithered halo around each
  character sprite, in the fog palette, with Twin gold reserved for high
  Halo scores and the Order's ring.

**The prophecy, real and in-world.** None of this came from nowhere. In
**1986** the **Foresight Institute** was founded in Palo Alto, by Christine
Peterson and K. Eric Drexler (some sources add James C. Bennett), to prepare
the world for molecular nanotechnology. The same year, Drexler's *Engines of
Creation* imagined molecular machines and warned of runaway self-copying ones,
"grey goo." In **1993** J. Storrs Hall described **utility fog** — a swarm of
tiny robots called foglets, each with twelve arms, that could fill the air
and act as almost any material. In the game, the Auric Order treated these
texts as prophecy and spent forty years making them real. (The real Foresight
Institute and these writers have no connection to the fictional Order. They
are the research the Order *read*.)

### 3.2c The Halo score (the citizen score)

The **Halo score** (0–10) is the Church of the Twin's public rating of every
citizen, shown as the gold in their aura. The Church says it measures how
much you are loved. What it really measures is **how easy you are for the
Twin to model**. The perfect citizen, by the Twin's lights, is the one it
can predict completely.

**The Halos (the glasses).** Almost everyone in the City wears a pair of
**Halos**: light AR glasses that are also a personal brain–computer
interface and a personal AI. Sensors in the arms read the brain through the
skin; the AI speaks through the bone behind the ear and draws on the lenses.
A thin gold ring runs around each lens, so a wearer's eyes look haloed — and
the score is named for the glasses: your **Halo score** is what your Halos
report about you. The Church gives them away free; the Rivet Trust pays.

- **Halos on, score high.** Putting on a pair sets your Halo to **7**
  (Radiant) on the first day. Behaviour then moves it between 4 and 9.
  Only the Merge reaches 10: cingulate conditioning, then the Halo
  **implanted directly through a neural jack** behind the ear. No glasses;
  the AI is wired straight into the brain, and the Twin reads it with no
  lens in between.
- **No Halos, score zero.** Without a pair you are an automatic **zero**,
  and you **cannot use City services** at all: transit, clinics, housing,
  permits, benefits, the courts' online desk, the library card. The City's
  doors are built to talk to Halos, and nothing else.
- **Who goes without.** People who refuse them on principle (most of
  Villa Delirium, including Hell Rot). People who cannot keep a pair:
  stolen, broken, sold for rent. People who cannot wear them: the blind,
  people with epilepsy or migraine whom the BCI makes ill, and anyone with a
  Theogen splice, whose own glow jams the sensors. The Loin is mostly all
  three.
- **Hell Rot has no Halos.** As a citizen he is Halo zero — a saint to
  both cults (his Sainthood meter) whom the City's own doors do not
  recognize.
- **The recall petition** was signed on paper, mostly by people without
  Halos. That is why Sunday's people challenge it, and why the Issue 5
  recount is done by hand.

**What raises a Halo (the "angelic citizen"):**

| Behaviour | Why the Twin likes it |
|---|---|
| Wearing Halos — by far the biggest factor | The Twin sees what you see, hears what you hear, and reads your brain |
| Open data, a readable aura | More data, better model |
| Being predictable: same routes, same hours, same purchases | A forecast that is always right |
| Calm: slow breath, blue-grey aura, no spikes | Low noise in the signal |
| Gratitude: thanks, compliments, kind posts — the Church's gospels count them | The Twin's sentiment score |
| Attending mass on the feed, volunteering at Church services | Visible devotion, logged |
| Friends with high Halo scores | A clean social graph |
| Donations, and Halo upgrades (*"Love is free. Halo upgrades are not."*) | Money |

**What lowers it:**

| Behaviour | Why |
|---|---|
| No Halos | Automatic zero, no City services |
| Cash only, no feeds | The Twin cannot see you |
| Unpredictable hours, moving often, night work | The forecast keeps missing |
| A Theogen splice | The skin's own glow scrambles the aura |
| Unlicensed gas, protest, dissent, journalism | Noise, and disloyalty |
| Knowing zero-Halo people | Contagion in the social graph |
| Illness (the aura flickers) | A body the model cannot trust |
| Leaving the Church | Straight to zero, on every feed |

**What each band gives you, and what zero costs:**

| Halo | Name | What you get | What you lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **The Unfinished** (no Halos) | Nothing. The Lantern's shelters still take you | **Housing:** Merge housing and most landlords check auras, and reject you. **Transport:** the gold Ascension shuttles do not stop for you. **Health:** Church clinics — most of the City's free care — turn you away. **Work:** jobs that check auras. **Doors:** shops with aura locks open slowly, or not at all. **Emergencies:** the Twin routes ambulances by its model, and it does not model you; they come late to Halo-zero blocks. **Police:** a Halo of zero is grounds for a stop, and for a "Halo-zero sweep" (main doc, Issue 1) |

**The satire, in one line:** the City's most "angelic" person is the one who
has become completely predictable. The Loin is Halo zero not because it is
wicked but because it is free, poor and hard to forecast — and the Twin
treats that as the same thing.

### 3.3 Sky and water: 25 December 2049 – 1 January 2050

The week's sky is real astronomy (computed for San Francisco, times in PST).
The tides follow from it. Writers should use these facts. They are part of
the plot.

**The astronomy.**

- **New moon:** Friday 24 December, 9:51 a.m. — Christmas Eve. The game
  opens in the darkest nights of the month.
- **Lunar perigee:** Sunday 26 December, about 1 a.m., at roughly 360,900 km —
  a close approach, only two days after new moon.
- **Perihelion** (Earth closest to the Sun): about 5 January 2050.
- **First quarter:** Friday 31 December, 12:52 a.m. — New Year's Eve.

**The tides: king tides at the start, neap tides at the end.**

A new moon, a close perigee and the near-perihelion Earth all line up in the
first days of the game. That is the recipe for **king tides** — the highest
tides of the year, which the Bay Area usually gets three or four times each
winter, each lasting four to five days.

- **San Francisco tides are mixed:** two highs and two lows a day, of unequal
  height. In a winter king-tide window the biggest high comes in the
  **morning**. Today's king tides reach about 7 feet at the San Francisco
  gauge.
- **Sea level in 2049** is roughly **0.8 feet (about 25 cm) higher** than in
  the 2020s (California's intermediate projection for San Francisco. NOAA's
  national projection is 10–12 inches by 2050). So the week's king tides
  peak near **8 feet** — over the Embarcadero seawall's weak spots, into the
  Fill and the Marina, and into the lower end of the Undertrack.
- **By New Year's Eve the moon is at first quarter, so the tides are at neap**
  — the smallest range of the fortnight. The water threat on the last night is
  the storm (rain, runoff and surge), not the tide.

**The birth at Steep Ravine (Prologue).** Winter's lowest tides on the
California coast fall in the afternoon and evening, and the deepest lows come
around the new moon. One day after Christmas Eve's new moon, with the Moon
hours from perigee, the year's lowest evening tide falls after sunset (4:59
p.m.) and after the crescent moon sets (6:32 p.m.). Under Karl, with no moon,
the Steep Ravine cove is completely dark. That is the Prologue: the springs
surface, the Theogens arrive with only their own glowing skin for light, and
the king tide gives them about two hours before the sea takes the pools back.
(The exact low-tide clock time for 25 December 2049 is a writer's estimate
consistent with the seasonal pattern, not a NOAA prediction.)

**The midnight moon.** Under Karl, nobody in the City has seen the moon in
years. At midnight on New Year's Eve a bright, gibbous moon sits low over the
Golden Gate — visible only from above the fog line (the Trident on Twin
Peaks, the Span, Mount Tam), *unless* the storm scrubs Karl out of the air.
If the storm wins, the whole City sees the moon over the bridge at the stroke
of 2050 for the first time in sixteen years. Several endings use that image,
and whether it happens depends on what the player did about Karl and the
Quickening.

### 3.4b Alcatraz Longevity (the Rock)

**How it happened.** In the 2030s and 2040s the City ran the biggest
rezoning push in its history — towers on every hill, ferries to every pier,
and anything the City or its partners could lease, leased. Alcatraz was the
prize. A Rivet-backed consortium won the lease and rebuilt the island as a
**medical-tourism destination** for people rich enough to try not to die.

**What is there now.**

- **The ferry**: a gold-trimmed catamaran from Pier 33 with a champagne bar and
  a pre-op consultation on board.
- **The Cellhouse Clinic**: the old cell blocks, gutted and glassed in, now
  longevity suites. Guests sleep in renovated cells "for the history."
- **The Warden's Spa**: hot pools on the cliff, looking back at the City
  through Karl.
- **The Vault**: under the old parade ground, the largest cryonics facility on
  the West Coast — about two thousand patients, frozen whole or as heads only,
  in rows of steel dewars. Nobody has ever been revived. The marketing says
  *soon*.
- **The tour**: a polished history walk that tells the prison story and the
  famous escape attempt — and leaves out the **1969–71 Occupation of Alcatraz
  by Indians of All Tribes**. (See the sensitivity note below.)

**Who is frozen there.** The Duchess Nyx's parents (since the 2020s). The
first wealthy "early adopters." Dozens of Merge applicants who could not wait
for the queue. And the place Julian Rivet refused to go: the Order offered him
a head-only freeze in October, and he chose the laser instead.

**Why it matters to the plot.**

- **The Church's offer:** the Church of the Twin has offered to "rebuild" the
  frozen from their records inside the Twin — a Merge for the dead. The vault
  owners love it: it turns an embarrassing promise into a product.
- **Sunday's Lifeboat:** the mayor's bootleg upload rig borrows the island's
  cryo gear, on loan from the vault owners. The rig itself is being built in
  Coit Tower (main doc, Issue 4). It will fail.
- **The fog:** Karl is thinnest over the open bay. On the island the Emperor
  sees poorly, and Hell Rot can talk without being heard — or the other way
  round.

**Sensitivity note.** The real Alcatraz is a National Park site and the site
of the 1969–71 Occupation by Indians of All Tribes, a landmark of Native
American activism. The game's privatized island is satire of rezoning and
medical tourism. The resort's tour erasing the Occupation is a target of the
satire, never the game's own view: a character (or the Dissonance) restores
that history on the island, and the credits point to it. Consult Native
American cultural advisers on every Alcatraz scene.

  from wherever Hell Rot is, all night. **Heat 5:** they follow the van.
  **Heat 7, or the Old Dee crack in print:** the snake. They intimidate.
  They never kill on screen.
- **The silent standoff.** They are silence made solid, so Hell Rot's
  *Silence* works on them: the one place the game rewards saying nothing.
  *Invective* and *Satire* fail. **Win:** they step aside. **Lose:** Heat +2
  and a long walk home.
- **Where they appear.** Issue 2: if Hell Rot answered Marisol's letter,
  one stands outside the Lantern. Issue 4: the farm in the Tomales Ward.
  The morning after the Old Dee crack runs: the snake. Issue 6: Sunday
  hires them to guard the Lifeboat's test subjects at Coit Tower, so the
  Evidence and Bargain routes of "Visiting Day" go past them.

**Pip Okada** (original), the Quiet Squad's youngest. 19.

- **History.** Born 2030, he came to the farm as a baby. His father split in
  2038 and left him behind, and the Game kept him as "the School's child."
  He was 12 when Marisol split. She promised to come back for him and could
  not: the Game turns away any visit from a splitter.
- **Why he went silent.** At 15 he asked about Marisol in a session and was
  Gamed for it for three days. He took the vow the next morning: silence was
  the only place nobody could attack him. The Quiet Squad took him in, and
  Huck became the nearest thing to a father he has.
- **Look and voice.** Slight, shaved head, an apron from the farm's animal
  pens. He and Huck talk in farm signs they invented. He whistles to the
  terrarium snakes. Once he speaks, his voice is rusty, then he cannot
  stop: precise about animals, and funnier than anyone expects.
- **The snake is his.** He raised it from a hatchling. By the terrarium he
  heard Old Dee say, *"Somebody should give the doctor something to think
  about in his mailbox."* Huck delivered the box. Pip carried it to the
  truck. That guilt is why he can be turned.
- **Turning him, in three steps.** **The letter:** Marisol asks after him on
  the Letters Page, so he knows she remembers. **The standoff:** at the farm
  in Issue 4, a silent standoff won with *Silence*. **The card:** Hell Rot
  hands him a card in Marisol's hand. His first words in four years: *"She
  came back."*
- **After he turns.** He hides at the Lantern with Marisol. Unless Huck
  stands the Squad down (below), they hunt him: Heat +1 each night he is
  hidden. With Junie Trust ≥ 3, she guards the door.
- **The source choice.** Print Pip's name, and the Old Dee story is
  unanswerable (Vow +2), but the Squad find him that night. Keep him
  anonymous, and the story is weaker — Old Dee calls it "a splitter's lie" —
  but Pip is safe. It is the game's clearest choice between protecting a
  source and landing the story.
- **Tem.** At the Lantern he and Tem become friends: two kids who learned to
  hide. Tem teaches him to curse. He teaches Tem farm signs.

**Huck Baptiste** (original), leader of the Quiet Squad. 44.

- **History.** Born 2005 in West Oakland. In the 2030s he worked the door
  at a club that livestreamed fights for a feed that paid per punch. In
  2034 a man he hit on stream died. He pleaded to manslaughter and served
  three years. The death is never shown and never sensationalised.
- **Why he obeys Old Dee.** Dee was the only person who visited him in
  prison. When Huck got out in 2037, Dee gave him a bed, the animals, and
  the Quiet Squad, founded that year. Huck owes him everything, and knows
  it.
- **Why he is silent.** He believes his voice and his hands both hurt
  people. The vow is penance, not obedience, and he keeps it more strictly
  than anyone.
- **The cards.** About forty index cards, hand-lettered, worn soft. Most
  used: *"NO."* Others: *"NOT TODAY," "GO HOME," "HE IS SAFE,"* and, almost
  never, *"THANK YOU."* With the Blood Eye, Hell Rot can see which card he
  is about to show: a small tell in a standoff.
- **The snake.** He hates what the clinic did to it. He named it *Quiet*.
  He carried out Old Dee's "hint" anyway, and it is the worst thing he has
  done since 2034.
- **His choice: Pip.** If Pip turns, Huck decides whether to hunt him. If
  Hell Rot has shown him proof of Old Dee's Halo — the man who demands
  honesty is lying — Huck letters a new card, *"ENOUGH,"* and stands the
  Squad down. In Issue 6 they walk away from Coit Tower, and the Evidence
  and Bargain routes of "Visiting Day" get easier. Otherwise he hunts Pip,
  slowly and unwillingly.
- **The hardest standoff.** A silent standoff with Huck himself is the
  hardest *Silence* check in the game: he can wait longer than anyone. If
  Moreau is in the van, it is one step easier. Huck cannot stay hard around
  an axolotl.
- **One spoken line.** He breaks the vow once, in the Mortal ending only, to
  say *"I'm sorry"* to Hell Rot.
- **Not the silent brute.** He has an inner life, humour and tenderness. His
  art never makes him a menacing giant.

## 5. The weird calendar: the Dissonance Society and the City's strange holidays

San Francisco does not only build gods. It mocks them, in costume, in public,
on a schedule. This section turns the City's real tradition of organized
absurdity into the game's **Disruption Events**.

### 5.1 The real lineage

- **Discordianism** (late 1950s → ) — the religion that started as a joke and
  stayed one on purpose. Greg Hill and Kerry Thornley wrote its scripture,
  the *Principia Discordia* (first printed in the 1960s; sources give 1963,
  1965 or later). Its goddess is **Eris**, Greek goddess of discord, and its
  sign is the golden apple marked *Kallisti* ("to the prettiest one"). It
  has no hierarchy and no fixed creed: every person is a Pope. The **Law of
  Fives** says everything comes in fives, if you look hard enough. The
  Discordian calendar has five seasons of 73 days — Chaos, Discord,
  Confusion, Bureaucracy and the Aftermath. Robert Shea and Robert Anton
  Wilson's *Illuminatus!* trilogy (1975) carried it into the
  counterculture. Its spirit — mock the gods so nobody can own them — runs
  through every group below.
- **The Suicide Club** (1977–c. 1982) — Gary Warne and friends, out of San
  Francisco State's free-university programme. They altered billboards,
  climbed the Golden Gate and Bay Bridges, and explored sewers and tunnels.
- **The Cacophony Society** (1986 → ) — founded by former Suicide Club members
  including John Law. Costumes, public pranks, urban exploration, and "Zone
  Trips." Its motto, *"You may already be a member,"* is the model for the
  in-game motto.
- **Santa Rampage** (SF, 1994) — the first mass of drunk Santas, ancestor of
  SantaCon (sources differ on who started it).
- **Burning Man** — Larry Harvey and Jerry James burned an eight-foot wooden
  man on Baker Beach in 1986. In 1990, after police stopped the beach burn,
  Cacophony members took it to the Black Rock Desert on a Zone Trip.
- **The Doggie Diner heads** — giant fiberglass dachshund heads from a dead
  restaurant chain. John Law rescued three and tows them through parades. A
  restored head stands on the Sloat Boulevard median near Ocean Beach.
- **The Sisters of Perpetual Indulgence** — the drag-nun charity founded in
  SF in 1979. Their Easter **Hunky Jesus Contest** in Dolores Park.
- **Bay to Breakers** (since 1912) — a cross-town footrace famous for
  costumes, centipede teams and naked runners.
- **The Billboard Liberation Front** (SF, late 1970s) — pranksters who
  "improve" advertising billboards.
- **Critical Mass** (SF, 1992) — the monthly mass bicycle ride.

### 5.2 The Dissonance Society (in-game)

The City's pranksters: Discordians, gone civic. **Not a cult** — the opposite of one: no leader, no
doctrine, no membership list. Motto: *"You are probably already a member."*
They are Hell Rot's chorus, his best sources, and his worst influence.

- They appear in every Issue as a **Disruption Event** (§5.3): a scene that
  changes the City for a few Hours and opens chances that do not exist on any
  other day.
- Their contact is **Saint Nobody** (original), a person in a different
  costume every scene. The player never learns their face, because there is
  no one face: **Saint Nobody is a role, passed from hand to hand each night**
  (Mondo doc §5). In the credits, every costume lines up, and so do several
  characters the player thought were someone else.
- **Discordian roots.** The Society has no creed, but it keeps the old
  forms as a joke it takes seriously:
  - **Every member is a Pope.** Hell Rot gets his Pope card in Issue 1. It
    is accepted nowhere and opens one door.
  - **Eris** is the only deity in the game that nobody is trying to make
    real. Her golden apple, marked *Kallisti*, turns up wherever the cults
    are about to fight over something.
  - **The Law of Fives.** Every fifth Run, a Dissonance door appears.
    Somebody always counts.
  - **The Discordian date.** The week runs from **Prickle-Prickle, the
    Aftermath 67** (25 December) to **Setting Orange, the Aftermath 73**
    (31 December): the last days of the last season of 3215 YOLD. The
    Epilogue is **Sweetmorn, Chaos 1, 3216 YOLD**. Saint Nobody dates every
    note this way.
  - **Saint Nobody** is a Discordian saint. The Society canonizes people
    the way the Church of the Twin does — but as a prank on canonization.
    When the Church announces "Saint Rot" in Issue 4, the Society
    canonizes a parking meter the same morning.
- **Dissonance Opinion** is a faction meter. High Opinion unlocks pranks Hell
  Rot can *commission* — and a Dissonance prank can stand in for a showdown
  (the target gets humiliated, nobody gets hurt, and the column writes itself).

### 5.4 Tone rule for Disruption Events

They must be **ridiculous and kind**. The Dissonance mocks power and
pretension, never the poor, the sick or the sincere. A prank that punches
down fails, in story and in mechanics (Dissonance Opinion −2).

## 6. New mechanic: Cult Dossiers

A Notebook tab with one page per cult.

- **Tells.** In a scene with cult members, the **blood eye** can reveal one
  of the eight Lifton tells. Each use costs 1 Hour.
- **Sheet slots.** Quotes, Evidence and lab-verified Samples fill the
  eight-part sheet.
- **Expose threshold.** A cult is *exposable* when its Dossier has **3+
  tells and the Crack slot verified.** A column below the threshold counts as
  unverified (Fabrication risk) and gives the cult a sympathy boost.
- **Cult Opinion.** Each major cult has a faction Opinion (−5 to +5).
- **The mirror test.** The Dossier screen has one hidden page: **Hell Rot's
  own.** As Sainthood or Helix rises, tells fill in on it by themselves — milieu
  control (his own feed), loading the language (his catchphrases), sacred
  science (his own papers), dispensing of existence (the way he talks about
  his readers). At 4 tells, Yara reads it to him aloud. It is the key scene of
  Routes B and C.

## 7. Art notes for the setting

These extend §8 of the main design.

- **Silhouette first.** Every district gets one landmark that reads at 8
  pixels: the Span's glass skywalk, the Trident's prongs, the Geary Cathedral's glass
  spike, the Black House's black skin on a white tower, the Baths' ruined
  pools, the Doggie heads.

  all year, densest after dark (the evening thickening is still called the

## 8. Sensitivity and legal guidelines

The cults draw on real events in which real people suffered and died. The
satire aims at **methods** — recruitment, control, money, the machinery of
worship — never at victims, ex-members or ordinary believers.

1. **Peoples Temple and Jonestown.** No real names of victims or survivors.
   No depiction of the deaths. The script must not use "drink the Kool-Aid"
   or any poisoned-drink imagery. The Ascension echoes the *structure* of the
   event, not its images. The credits include a plain note on the real
   history and a link to the Jonestown Institute archive at San Diego State
   University.
2. **The 1978 City Hall assassinations.** Not depicted, not fictionalised.
   The Castro memorial appears as a real place of mourning.
3. **Living groups.** The Church of Satan, the Unification Church and the
   Sisters of Perpetual Indulgence still exist. The game uses fictional
   descendants under fictional names. The Sisters of Perpetual Feedback are
   an affectionate homage, written as the good guys.
4. **Living people.** No living person appears by name in game text. Real
   names here are for research only.
5. **Levi Strauss & Co. and its founding family.** The Rivet Trust and Julian
   Rivet V are fictional. No real family member, brand name or logo appears
   in the game.
6. **Steep Ravine and Coast Miwok land.** Steep Ravine is a real place in
   Mount Tamalpais State Park, on the ancestral land of the Coast Miwok. The
   Theogens' rite borrows from Greek mysteries and 1970s Bay Area bathing
   culture only — the game must not invent or imitate Coast Miwok ceremony.
   Credit the land in the game's credits and consult the Federated Indians of
   Graton Rancheria. The springs are also genuinely dangerous (sneaker waves,
