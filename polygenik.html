The Chinese
Peptide Rave

On December 13, 2025, we turned the 8th‑floor lab at Frontier Tower into a nightclub for gray‑market biology. A field trip through live experiments, then a DJ spinning techno while molecular structures strobed across the wall.

A mix‑your‑own peptides workshop. BPC‑157, TB‑500, epitalon, retatrutide — the alphabet soup Silicon Valley is injecting faster than the FDA can spell it. Dress code: crazy futuristic cyberpunk. Tall shiny boots and light‑up headwear encouraged. Trash fits turned away at the door.

Then The New York Times showed up, and our little rave became the face of a movement.

"A mix‑your‑own peptides workshop, a D.J. playing techno with chemistry structures projected in the background and a dress code calling for crazy futuristic cyberpunk attire." — The New York Times, Jan 3 2026

The Celebrity
Meat Tasting

A dinner party where the menu is people — consensually. Cultivated meat grown from celebrity cells, plated, seasoned, and served to a room willing to ask the question out loud: would you take a bite?

Cell‑cultured meat means we no longer need to kill an animal to eat one. It also means we could, in theory, culture anyone. The technology is real. The consent is the art. The tasting is where the debate gets a fork.

It's not cannibalism if the cells were donated — technically. That "technically" is the entire exhibit.

"What sacrifices do we need to make to keep eating meat at the pace we do — and who will be able to afford animal meat, versus who may have no option but to culture it from themselves?" — The question behind Ouroboros Steak & celebrity‑meat art

The
Lineage

We didn't invent trouble. We're just running it through a bioreactor.

2019 — · BROOKLYN

MSCHF

Numbered "drops" · Satan Shoes · Big Red Boots

The blueprint: release provocations as scarce, numbered products and let the internet do the arguing. Critique capitalism by out‑performing it. We swapped sneakers for cell cultures.

2014 — · CONCEPT

BiteLabs

Salami from A‑list tissue

A slick startup pitch to grow celebrity salami — half hoax, half market test. It proved a website and a provocation could force a global conversation about celebrity, consent, and appetite.

2020 — · DESIGN MUSEUM

Ouroboros Steak

Telhan · Pelling · Knight

A grow‑your‑own kit for steaks made from your own cells — "technically" not cannibalism. Nominated for Design of the Year. The direct ancestor of our tasting menu.

2003 — · SYMBIOTICA

Disembodied Cuisine

Oron Catts & Ionat Zurr

Frog steaks grown in a gallery bioreactor, then cooked and eaten — the first humans to eat in‑vitro meat, a decade before the lab‑grown burger. They coined the "semi‑living" and put biology on the gallery wall. Our tasting menu's true ancestor.

We operate out of a vertical village.

Polygenik is based at Frontier Tower — sixteen floors of deep‑tech founders, biohackers and artists at 995 Market Street, San Francisco. Our lab lives on the 8th floor. Some nights it's a lab. Some nights it's a dancefloor. The difference is mostly the lighting.

We believe the most important biotech debates shouldn't happen in a subcommittee — they should happen at a party, with the actual technology in the room. So we run the real experiment, throw the doors open, and turn the ethics into an evening you can't stop talking about.

Founded in the tradition of the biopunk and DIY‑bio movements: curiosity first, permission later, spectacle always.
